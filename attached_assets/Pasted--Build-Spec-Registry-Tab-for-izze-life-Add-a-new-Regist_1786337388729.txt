# Build Spec — Registry Tab for izze.life

Add a new **Registry** tab/page to the existing izze.life wedding website.

Before implementing anything, inspect the existing izze.life codebase and reuse its current design system, navigation, typography, colors, spacing, responsive behavior, components, and interaction patterns wherever possible.

The Registry should feel completely native to izze.life.

It should **not** look or feel like:

* a third-party wedding registry
* an online store
* a checkout
* a crowdfunding campaign
* a fintech product

The experience should be warm, personal, editorial, minimal, and slightly playful.

There is no backend, database, gift tracking, payment tracking, reconciliation, or payment verification.

---

# 1. Core Concept

The Registry has two parts:

### Primary request: Bring us a book

We want every guest, if they can, to bring a book for either Izzy or Zé that will eventually become part of our home library.

### Optional monetary gift: Help us decide our next step

Guests who still want to give us something beyond the book can make a monetary gift.

Instead of funding traditional wedding-registry categories such as a honeymoon or a house, the monetary gift becomes a playful **vote for where Izzy & Zé should live next**.

There are exactly four options:

**🌲 Plant roots in Seattle**

**🇵🇹 Move back to Portugal**

**🇲🇽 Move back to Mexico**

**🗽 Move back to New York**

The amount a guest chooses does not need to be recorded or actually counted as a vote.

There is no leaderboard or voting backend.

“Voting” is simply the framing of the gift experience.

---

# 2. Overall Flow

The Registry should use a focused, multi-step experience.

The flow is:

**Registry Home**

→ **Gift / Vote**

→ **Amount**

→ **Payment**

→ **Payment Details**

If Bank Transfer is selected:

**Registry Home**

→ **Gift / Vote**

→ **Amount**

→ **Payment**

→ **Bank Transfer**

→ **USA / Portugal / International**

Each step should **replace the previous view**.

Do not use accordions.

Do not reveal the next step underneath the current step.

Do not create one long page where each selection expands additional content below it.

---

# 3. Registry Home

When someone first opens the Registry tab, show **no monetary gift options**.

The initial page should feel like a personal note rather than a registry checkout.

## Header

**Our Registry**

Supporting copy:

“We have one small favor before anything else.”

# 4. Book Request

The book request should be the emotional centerpiece of the Registry Home.

Create a prominent but elegant card or editorial section.

A tasteful book/library icon such as 📚 can be used, provided it fits the existing izze.life visual language.

Small eyebrow:

**First things first**

Heading:

## Bring us a book

Copy:

“We'd love for each guest to bring a book for either Izzy or Zé — something you love, something that made you think, or something you think belongs on our shelf.

One day, when we finally settle down somewhere, we'll use them to build our home library — with a little piece of all of you in it.”

The tone should feel sentimental and personal.

This is the primary registry request.

# 5. “Your Presence Is the Gift” Section

Below the book request, create a second section.

Heading:

## And yes, your presence really is the gift.

If you'd still like to give us something beyond a book, we'd be incredibly grateful.”

Then show one primary CTA:

### Important

Do not show:

**I'd also like to give a gift**

# 11. Gift Selection Layout

On desktop, display the four options as a clean **2 × 2 grid**.

For example:

**Plant roots in Seattle** | **Move back to Portugal**

**Move back to Mexico** | **Move back to New York**

All four cards should be equal in size.

On mobile, stack them vertically or use another responsive layout that preserves readability and tap targets.

Do not make any destination look like the preferred/default choice.

No option should be preselected.

---

# 12. Amount Step

After a guest selects one of the four options, advance to a dedicated Amount view.

Breadcrumb:

**Registry › Gift › Amount**

Carry the selected destination forward.

For example:

“You're voting to help us…”

## Move back to Portugal

Or:

“You're voting to help us…”

## Plant roots in Seattle

Then:

**Enter your contribution**

Display one blank amount field with a fixed Euro symbol.

Example:

**€ [                    ]**

### Amount Rules

* Currency is EUR.
* Input must be blank by default.
* Do not prepopulate an amount.
* Do not show suggested amounts.
* Do not show €50 / €100 / €250 / €500 buttons.
* Do not show minimum contribution suggestions.
* Accept positive numerical values only.
* Use the numeric keyboard on mobile.
* Do not allow Continue until an amount is entered.

CTA:

**Continue**

Clicking Continue advances to Payment.

# 25. End of Payment Flow

The payment details page is the final functional step.

There is no need for the guest to return and tell the website that they paid.

Do not include a button that claims:

**Payment complete**

or:

**Payment successful**

There is no payment verification.

At the bottom of each payment-details screen, provide:

**← Back to Registry**

Optionally include a subtle closing message:

**Thank you ❤️**

“We can't wait to celebrate with you in Évora.”

This is simply a friendly closing message.

It does not indicate that payment was received.

Breadcrumbs should evolve as the guest advances.

Examples:

**Registry › Gift**

**Registry › Gift › Amount**

**Registry › Gift › Amount › Payment**

**Registry › Gift › Amount › Payment › Venmo**

For bank transfer:

**Registry › Gift › Amount › Payment › Bank Transfer**

then:

**Registry › Gift › Amount › Payment › Bank Transfer › Portugal**

Earlier breadcrumb items should be clickable where practical.

Going backward should preserve temporary selections during the current browser session.

---

# 27. Interaction Model

Every major decision advances to a new view.

For example:

Guest selects:

**Move back to Mexico**

→ replace current view with Amount.

Guest enters:

**€150**

and clicks Continue

→ replace Amount with Payment.

Guest selects:

**Bank Transfer**

→ replace Payment with Bank Transfer Location.

Guest selects:

**Portugal**

→ replace location selection with Portuguese Bank Details.

Do not:

* append new sections below existing sections
* use accordions for the main flow
* create a continuously expanding page
* display multiple steps simultaneously

The flow should feel calm and focused.

---

# 28. Frontend State Only

Maintain only the temporary frontend state necessary for navigation.

For example:

`giftVote`

`amount`

`paymentMethod`

`bankRegion`

Example:

`giftVote = "portugal"`

`amount = 250`

`paymentMethod = "bank"`

`bankRegion = "portugal"`

This state exists only to render the correct screens during the guest's current interaction.

It does not need to survive a page refresh.

---

# 29. No Tracking

This is extremely important.

There is **no actual voting system**.

Do not track votes.

Do not track money.

Do not store anything about individual guests.

Do NOT:

* create a database
* save destination selections
* save contribution amounts
* calculate votes
* calculate destination totals
* calculate percentages
* create a Seattle vs Portugal vs Mexico vs New York leaderboard
* store payment methods
* track whether a guest paid
* reconcile payments
* create gift records
* create user accounts
* collect names
* collect email addresses
* build payment-provider API integrations
* build webhooks
* build an admin dashboard

“Help us decide our next step” and “vote” are simply playful UX framing.

The actual transfer happens independently through the guest's chosen payment method.

---

# 30. No Payment Processing

izze.life is not processing payments.

The site only provides instructions or redirects the guest to the appropriate payment app.

Actual payments happen through:

* Venmo
* MB WAY
* Revolut
* Bank Transfer

Do not build Stripe or another payment-processing layer.

Do not handle card details.

Do not handle Apple Pay.

Do not create a checkout.

---

# 31. Payment Credentials

Do not show payment credentials until the guest reaches the relevant details page.

For example:

Venmo information appears only after selecting Venmo.

MB WAY phone number appears only after selecting MB WAY.

US bank details appear only after:

**Bank Transfer → USA**

Portuguese bank details appear only after:

**Bank Transfer → Portugal**

International banking information appears only after:

**Bank Transfer → International**

Keep the actual credentials in a clearly identifiable configuration/constants location so they can easily be updated later.

Use placeholders initially.

This is primarily about keeping the page clean and avoiding unnecessarily exposing every payment identifier at once. It should not be treated as a security mechanism.

---

# 32. Responsive Design

Prioritize mobile.

Many guests will access the Registry from their phones immediately before or during the wedding.

On mobile:

* stack the four destination cards appropriately
* keep tap targets large
* use a numeric keyboard for the amount
* ensure long IBAN/account numbers do not overflow
* make Copy controls obvious
* keep breadcrumbs usable
* keep payment app buttons prominent
* preserve generous whitespace

On desktop:

Use a **2 × 2 grid** for the four destination choices.

---

# 33. Visual Direction

Match the existing izze.life design system exactly.

Reuse:

* existing fonts
* existing colors
* existing navigation
* existing page width
* existing spacing
* existing button styles
* existing border radius
* existing cards
* existing responsive breakpoints
* existing animation conventions

Do not create a separate Registry design system.

The visual character should be:

**Elegant**

**Warm**

**Personal**

**Editorial**

**Minimal**

**Premium**

with small playful moments.

The book request should feel sentimental.

The destination vote should feel playful.

The payment screens should feel simple and trustworthy without looking like fintech software.

---

# 34. Emotional Hierarchy

The page should communicate these ideas in this exact order:

### 1. We want a book from you.

This is the meaningful, personal gift we genuinely want guests to bring.

### 2. Your presence is genuinely enough.

Make the usual wedding-registry cliché self-aware and funny.

### 3. But if you really want to give us something else...

The guest actively chooses:

**I'd also like to give a gift**

### 4. Help us decide what comes next.

Instead of buying us plates or funding a honeymoon, guests get to playfully advocate for the next chapter of our lives.

The four choices are:

🌲 **Plant roots in Seattle**

🇵🇹 **Move back to Portugal**

🇲🇽 **Move back to Mexico**

🗽 **Move back to New York**

### 5. Choose an amount.

No suggested amount.

### 6. Choose the easiest way to send it.

Venmo / MB WAY / Revolut / Bank Transfer.

This emotional hierarchy is central to the experience.
